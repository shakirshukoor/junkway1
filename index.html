Junk removal tips and guides

How much does junk removal cost in Halifax?

Most Halifax junk removal companies, including JunkWay Halifax, price by volume: you pay for the space your items take up in the truck. A mattress takes very little space. A full garage cleanout can fill most of a truck.

  • Minimum load: one or two pieces, like a mattress or an appliance.
  • Quarter truck: a room’s worth of furniture and boxes.
  • Half truck: a small basement or a large room plus extras.
  • Full truck: most of a house, an estate or a big renovation.

What changes the price

Volume matters most. Heavy or awkward items, stairs, special disposal (like fridges) and unsorted piles can add time.

Estate cleanout checklist for Halifax families
  1. Check legal authority. Confirm who has authority over the home and its contents before discarding anything. A lawyer can advise you; this is general information, not legal advice.
  2. Find important items first: documents, jewellery, cash, photos and keys. Check drawers, coats and freezers.
  3. Sort into four piles: keep, sell, donate, remove.
  4. Offer items to family and friends with a set deadline.
  5. Book the haul-away for what remains. We load everything and sweep up.
  6. Do a final walk-through of closets, attic, basement, shed and garage.

If the home is going on the market, clear it before photos and showings.

Junk removal vs. bin rental vs. a dump run
  • Junk removal: the crew does the lifting, and you spend a few minutes. Best for furniture, mixed junk and cleanouts.
  • Bin rental: you load it yourself over days or weeks. Best for long renovations. Check placement rules and extra fees.
  • DIY dump run: cheapest for a small load if you own a truck. Add fuel, tipping fees, your time and the risk of injury.

For heavy items, tight timelines or mixed loads, a haul-away usually saves the most time and effort. Send us a photo and compare.

Donate, recycle or dispose: what goes where?
  • Donate furniture, housewares, clothing, books and tools in good shape. Call ahead, since charities are selective.
  • Recycle TVs, computers and other electronics through an approved program. Never put them in the garbage.
  • Handle hazardous items like paint, chemicals and propane through a household hazardous waste depot.
  • Check your municipality’s collection rules for bulky items before you book a haul-away.

For large amounts or mixed loads, call us. We sort what we collect and send usable and recyclable items on where we can.
